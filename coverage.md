## Conventions for an entry

- **Read?** distinguishes items I actually fetched and read from items logged off a
  search result. An unread row is a lead, not a summary, and its Note is provisional.
- **Engagement** is the column that matters for this project: *model* means it engages
  the mechanics, the equations or the calibration; *numbers* means it uses the headline
  figures correctly but does not look inside; *framing* means the paper is a hook for a
  piece about something else.

## Claims circulating in coverage that the paper does not support

Checked against the paper, since this is where a reproduction has something to say.

1. **"Peer reviewers" or "external reviewers vetted it."** The paper's first-page
   footnote thanks 22 named economists, among them Acemoglu, Autor, Restrepo, Romer,
   Nakamura, Klenow, Moll and Steinsson, "for insightful comments on an earlier version".
   That is an acknowledgement, not review, and the landing page says outright that
   reviewers were not asked to endorse the conclusions. Coverage that presents the list
   as vetting overstates it. Verified against `paper.pdf` p. 1.
2. **Level gaps reported as growth rates.** The headline 1.6 / 8.3 / 32.4 percent are
   2030 GDP *levels* relative to a no-AI path, not growth rates. Several outlets, and
   the landing page's own "+32.4% GDP growth" label, blur this; Euronews's "15% growth"
   is a third quantity again, the extreme scenario's 2030 annual rate. The deck's backup
   slide on growth rates against level gaps exists precisely for this.

## What to watch

- A **Version 1.1** of the PDF. The landing page is explicitly versioned, so a revision
  is likely and would need re-checking against the reproduction.
- A **replication package**. None so far, which is the reason this project exists.
- Whether any acknowledged economist publishes **their own** response, rather than being
  cited as a reviewer. Acemoglu, Autor and Restrepo are the ones to watch.
- **Peer-reviewed placement** of the paper or a descendant of it.
- The **Alex Imas** piece, and any serious critique of the innovation block, which is
  where the "it understates AI" objection lands.
